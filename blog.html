TEJ7 Aviator Game – How the Crash Game Works and Key Tips for 2026

November 2026  ·  Game Guide

The Aviator crash game is one of TEJ7's most-played titles, and for good reason — it moves fast, the mechanics are transparent, and experienced players can run a structured session using dual bets. This guide explains exactly how Aviator works on TEJ7, what the multiplier curve actually means, and practical approaches that veteran players use to manage a session.

Aviator starts each round at a 1× multiplier. A plane graphic climbs the screen as the multiplier rises. The round ends at a random point determined by the server — the moment the plane "flies away." If you have not cashed out before that point, the stake for that round is lost. The result is determined before the round starts; the animation simply reveals it in real time.

TEJ7 supports dual bets in Aviator, which is one of its most useful features for structured play. With two active bets in the same round, a common approach is to set one at a conservative target (1.5× to 2×) and let the second run longer. The first cashout covers the round's cost; the second targets the upside. This is not a guaranteed return strategy — no strategy is in a crash game — but it gives you a disciplined framework instead of guessing at each round individually.

The round history panel on the TEJ7 Aviator screen shows the last 20 or so results. Keep in mind that each result is statistically independent. A run of low multipliers does not "predict" a high one coming next. Plan your session around total rounds and total budget, not round-to-round chasing.

To initiate a withdrawal: open TEJ7 → Wallet → Withdraw → select UPI → enter your UPI ID → enter the amount. Double-check the UPI ID before submitting. If your withdrawal is pending for more than 2 hours: check your TEJ7 transaction history to confirm the request was received. If it shows as pending, contact TEJ7 live support via in-app chat with your transaction ID. If it shows as completed but money has not arrived, check your UPI app's incoming transactions — sometimes bank-side processing adds a small delay even after TEJ7 completes their end.

TEJ7 Card Games Guide – Variants, Stakes and Skill Factors

October 2026  ·  Game Guide

Card games are the centrepiece of the TEJ7 lobby. The platform offers a wide spread of three-card variants alongside skill-based formats like Rummy, all running on live multiplayer tables. This guide breaks down each category, what makes them different, and what to know before sitting at a table.

Three-card formats on TEJ7 include Classic, Joker, AK47, Muflis, and 20-20 variants. Each has a different hand ranking system — Muflis, for example, inverts the standard ranking so the worst traditional hand becomes the best. If you are moving between variants, make sure you check the hand ranking rules before playing, as this catches many players off guard.

Rummy on TEJ7 follows the standard 13-card format with points-based scoring. Unlike crash games or colour prediction, Rummy involves a significant skill component — managing your hand, tracking discards, and reading opponent behaviour meaningfully affects outcomes over a session. Points Rummy moves the fastest; Deals Rummy is better if you prefer a fixed number of rounds with a defined endpoint.

Andar Bahar and Dragon Tiger are the fastest-moving formats on TEJ7's card table section. Both are single-decision per round — Andar or Bahar, Dragon or Tiger — with results delivered in seconds. These suit players who want rapid rounds with no complex decision-making between hands.
